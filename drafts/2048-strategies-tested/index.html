We Tested Every 2048 Strategy. Here's What Actually Wins

Every 2048 guide, including ours, hands out the same advice: pick a corner, build a snake, never swipe up. We wanted to know how much of that holds up, so we wrote six players — from button-mashing to a bot that thinks ahead — and let them play 400,600 games under the exact rules of our 2048. Some of the folklore survived. One famous rule did not.

The six players

Four of them follow fixed recipes — the kind of rule you could tape to your monitor. Two of them think.

  • Random presses a random arrow that moves something. It's the baseline: what the game gives you for free.
  • Corner recipe is the classic advice applied mechanically: swipe down if that moves anything, otherwise right, otherwise left, and up only when nothing else moves. Its biggest tile drifts into the bottom-right corner and mostly stays there.
  • Down–right spam alternates down and right, falling back to left and then up. It's the trick people share as a "cheat".
  • Greedy looks at all four moves and takes whichever merges the most points right now.
  • Lookahead considers each move, then every tile the game could spawn afterwards — a 2 with 90% probability or a 4 with 10%, in every empty cell — then its own best reply, and so on, two moves deep. It scores the positions it reaches the way a strong player judges a board: empty cells, tiles lined up in order, equal neighbours ready to merge.
  • Lookahead, no up is the same brain with one rule added: it may swipe up only when no other move is legal. It exists to test the most repeated tip in 2048.

Rules simulated. 4×4 board, two starting tiles, each new tile a 2 with 90% probability or a 4 with 10%, placed in a random empty cell — exactly as in our game. 100,000 games for each fixed recipe. The lookahead players are slow, so they played 300 and 300 games and stopped once they made 4096, which is why their scores aren't comparable with a full run.

The headline is stark: across 400,000 games, not one fixed recipe reached 2048. The corner recipe made 1024 in 4 games out of 100,000; down–right spam never did; greedy, the best of them, managed it 244 times — 0.24%.

That doesn't make the corner useless. Compare the corner recipe with random play: it reaches 256 in 51% of games against 8%, and 512 in 4.7% of games against 0.02%. Keeping the big tile parked is worth a lot. But a rule about which direction to press can't see what the press will do. Sooner or later it slides a 2 into the one gap your row needed, and nothing in the recipe notices. The tip "spam down and right" is the purest version of this, and it's no better than the corner recipe — 3.5% of games to 512.

Looking two moves ahead changes everything

The lookahead player won 297 of its 300 games — 99% to 2048 — and went on to 4096 in 96% of them. It isn't using some exotic trick. It's asking, before every swipe, "what's the worst likely thing that spawns next, and can I handle it?" — the question the recipes never ask.

It also tells us how long a game really is. The lookahead player needed a median of 964 moves to make 2048, never fewer than 922. That isn't a quirk of the bot — it's arithmetic. Merging never changes the total of the tiles on the board; only spawns add to it, and each spawn adds 2.2 on average. To hold a 2048 tile, the board must total at least 2048, which takes around 930 spawns on average, one per move. If you're 300 moves into a game and wondering why 2048 feels far away, it's because you're a third of the way there. Its score when the 2048 tile appeared was about 20,300, and reaching 4096 took around 1,900 moves and 44,500 points.

"Never swipe up" costs you games

Now the experiment we built this for. We took the lookahead player and forbade it from swiping up unless no other move was legal — exactly the rule from every beginner guide, our own included.

Banning up cost it 24 points of win rate to 2048 and 56 points to 4096. The unrestricted player used all four directions almost equally — 25% up, 25% down, 26% left, 24% right — and still kept its biggest tile in a corner after 99.6% of its moves. It wasn't even loyal to one corner: in 68% of the games we tracked it moved the big tile to a different corner at least once, up to 4 times in a game.

That's the real lesson hiding inside the folklore. What matters is not a direction but a shape: the biggest tile in a corner, the next ones lined up beside it in order. "Never swipe up" is a proxy for that shape — a rule simple enough to follow without thinking, which is exactly why it's a good rule for a beginner. A player who can look ahead doesn't need the proxy. They swipe up whenever up keeps the shape intact, and that flexibility is worth a lot of games.

What to do with this

  • Learn the corner, then stop thinking in directions. The corner and the snake are about where tiles sit. If a swipe keeps your big tile cornered and your row in order, it's a good swipe, whichever arrow it is.
  • Before each swipe, ask about the spawn. That one habit is the whole difference between the recipes and the lookahead player. Where could the new tile land? If a 2 dropped into the worst empty cell, could you still merge it?
  • Keep "never up" while you're learning. For a player who can't yet look ahead, a firm rule beats improvisation — the corner recipe reaches 256 6.5 times as often as random play. Drop the rule once you're reading the board, as in when to break the snake.
  • Expect about a thousand moves. A win takes roughly 930 spawns no matter how well you play. Patience isn't a virtue in 2048; it's arithmetic.
